The default principle under the **General Financial Rules (GFR) 2017** and the **Chhattisgarh Public Procurement Rules (CPPR) 2018** is to ensure fair competition. This means a department must explore all available options to get the best price and quality.

    *   **Request for Quotation (RFQ):** For purchases up to ₹2.5 lakh (or as per state-specific limits).
    *   **Open Tender (ATE):** For purchases above ₹2.5 lakh.

### 2. The Exception: Purchase of Proprietary Items (GFR Rule 154)

The only time a department can legitimately restrict the purchase of spare parts to the OEM is when the part is of a **"proprietary nature."**

**GFR Rule 154: Purchase of Goods of Proprietary Nature**

This rule allows for a deviation from open competition under strict conditions. A spare part can be considered proprietary if:

*   It is protected by a patent, copyright, or trademark.
*   It is specifically designed and manufactured by the OEM for use only with their main equipment.
*   It is not available in the open market from any other manufacturer or supplier.

**Conditions for Purchase under GFR Rule 154:**

1.  **Justification is Mandatory:** The department must prepare a formal justification explaining why the spare part is of a proprietary nature and why it cannot be sourced from the open market.
2.  **Record of Original Purchase:** The department must have a record that the main equipment itself was purchased from the OEM.
3.  **After-Sales Service:** The purchase must be for the purpose of maintenance, repair, or after-sales service of that specific equipment.

If the spare part is a standard, off-the-shelf item (e.g., a common filter, a standard bolt, a generic electronic component), it is **not** proprietary. In this case:

*   The department **cannot** restrict the purchase to the OEM.
*   It **must** follow the standard competitive bidding process (RFQ or Open Tender).
*   Attempting to force a purchase from the OEM without a valid proprietary justification would be a violation of procurement rules and could be challenged.

The primary rule governing this is **GFR Rule 161: Splitting of Procurement**.

*   **What it says:** This rule states that procurement of goods or services shall not be split into smaller quantities or lots to avoid the requirement of a tender, unless the procurement is for different items or services that are not related to each other.

### **Step-by-Step Guide to Estimating Total Procurement Value**

#### **Step 1: Define the Scope of Work (SoW) and Technical Specifications**

*   Exact quantity and specifications of the goods/services required.
*   Delivery schedule and location.
*   Installation, commissioning, and testing requirements.
*   Training, warranty, and after-sales support obligations.

#### **Step 2: Calculate the Base Cost of Goods/Services**

*   **Formula:** (Unit Price of Item/Service) x (Total Quantity Required)
*   **Source:** Use rates from previous contracts, market surveys, or price lists from vendors.

*   **Training and Documentation:** If the supplier is required to provide training to staff or supply technical manuals, these costs must be factored in.
*   **Other Charges:** Include any other costs like customs duty (for imports), local taxes, or bank charges.

#### **Step 4: Arrive at the Final Estimated Value**
Sum up all the costs from the steps above to get the final, comprehensive estimated value.

### Key Difference at a Glance

### Detailed Breakdown

#### 1. Administrative Approval (or Administrative Sanction)

This is the first step in the procurement process. It is an internal approval that validates the proposal to purchase goods or services.

*   **Purpose:** To ensure the purchase is for a legitimate public purpose, is technically feasible, and is not frivolous or redundant. It establishes the *necessity* of the expenditure.
*   **Key Questions Answered:**
    *   Is this item/service required for the functioning of the department?
    *   Are the specifications and quantity reasonable?
    *   Has due diligence been done to explore alternatives (e.g., repair vs. replace)?
    *   Is the purchase aligned with the department's objectives?
*   **Who Grants It:** The designated **Administrative Sanctioning Authority**, typically the Head of the Department (HoD) or a senior officer delegated this power.
*   **Regulatory Basis:**
    *   While the term "Administrative Approval" is widely used, the principle is embedded in the justification required for any expenditure. **GFR 2017, Rule 149** discusses the powers of sanctioning authorities, which inherently includes the power to assess the administrative necessity of a proposal.
    *   **Chhattisgarh Public Procurement Rules (CPPR) 2018, Rule 4** deals with "Sanctioning and Financial Powers" and outlines the responsibilities of the sanctioning authority, which includes ensuring the proposal is administratively sound before seeking financial approval.

#### 2. Financial Sanction

This is the second, and crucial, step that authorises the actual spending of government money.

*   **Purpose:** To formally approve the expenditure from the government's consolidated fund. It confirms that the budget has the necessary allocation and that the proposed amount is within the financial powers of the sanctioning authority.
*   **Key Questions Answered:**
    *   Is there sufficient unspent balance in the specific budget head (e.g., Major Head 4202, Minor Head 01, Sub-Head 101)?
    *   Is the total estimated cost within the financial limits delegated to this officer?
    *   Has the proposal been properly justified and vetted?
*   **Who Grants It:** The **Financial Sanctioning Authority**. This can be the same person as the Administrative Authority if they have been delegated financial powers, or it could be a higher authority like the Finance Secretary or the Minister-in-charge, depending on the amount.
*   **Regulatory Basis:**
    *   **GFR 2017, Rule 149** explicitly defines **"Financial Sanction"** as: *"The authority to incur an expenditure which is necessary to give effect to an administrative approval."* This rule clearly establishes the sequence and dependency.
    *   **GFR 2017, Rule 150** ("Sanctioning of Expenditure") further details that no expenditure can be incurred without a valid financial sanction. It also specifies the limits of financial powers for different authorities.
    *   **CG Treasury Rules** operationalise this by requiring a formally issued sanction order, often with a specific sanction number, before any payment can be processed by the treasury.

*   **Natural Disasters:** Procuring relief materials immediately after a flood or earthquake.

### **Actionable Steps for Writing Neutral Specifications**

#### **1. Use Performance-Based and Functional Specifications**

*   **GFR Rule 160(2):** Explicitly states that specifications should not be written in a manner that favours a particular manufacturer or supplier. It mandates that specifications should be based on the required performance and function.

*   **Instead of:** "Use XYZ brand's high-strength epoxy adhesive."

**Regulatory Basis:**
*   **GFR Rule 160(1):** Requires specifications to be "as precise as possible." Using standard codes and generic terms is the most precise and objective way to define a requirement.
*   **GeM Guidelines:** The Government e-Marketplace operates on a catalog of standardized, pre-defined specifications. Using GeM for procurement is the easiest way to ensure neutrality, as the specifications are already vetted and standardized.

#### **3. Avoid Unnecessary or Restrictive Details**

Do not include details that are not essential for the item's function or that only one vendor can meet.

*   **Avoid:** Specifying a particular colour, finish, or minor feature that is not critical to performance.
*   **Example:** If the colour of a server cabinet is not functionally important, do not specify "Midnight Black." Simply state "Standard 19-inch rack-mountable server cabinet."

**Regulatory Basis:**
*   **GFR Rule 160:** The rule warns against specifications that are restrictive and limit competition. Every requirement in the technical specification must be justifiable and linked to the item's intended use.

*   **How to Justify:**

### 2. Regulatory Citations

#### **General Financial Rules (GFR) 2017**

*   **Section 4.2.3 (Opening of Financial Bids):** This section confirms that financial bids are opened **only for the technically responsive bidders**. The financial bids of non-responsive bidders are not to be opened under any circumstances.

### 3. The Correct Procurement Process

1.  **Opening of Technical Bids:** All technical bids are opened in the presence of bidders or their representatives.
2.  **Evaluation of Technical Bids:** A committee evaluates each technical bid against the pre-defined technical criteria (e.g., experience, qualifications, certifications, manufacturing capacity).
3.  **Identification of Responsive Bidders:** Bidders who meet all the essential technical criteria are declared "responsive."
4.  **Disqualification of Non-Responsive Bidders:** All bidders who fail to meet the technical criteria are immediately disqualified. Their financial bids are **not opened**.
5.  **Opening of Financial Bids:** The financial bids of only the technically responsive bidders are opened.
6.  **Determination of L1:** The L1 (lowest bidder) is determined from the financial bids of the technically responsive bidders.

On the Government e-Marketplace (GeM), this principle is enforced automatically. If a bidder does not meet the required technical specifications (e.g., lacks a mandatory ISO certification, has insufficient turnover, or fails to upload required documents), the system automatically rejects the bid. The price is never even considered for such a bidder.

### Conclusion

The "lowest price" (L1) is only valid among bidders who are **technically compliant and responsive**. A technically non-responsive bidder is ineligible to participate in the financial evaluation stage. Selecting such a bidder would be illegal, arbitrary, and against the spirit of transparent and fair procurement as mandated by **GFR Rule 164** and **CPPR 2018**.

*   **GeM Buyer's Manual:** The manual states that once a tender is closed for bidding, the terms are locked. *   **What it is:** The official bill from the supplier, detailing the items supplied, quantities, agreed-upon prices, and applicable taxes (like GST).

*   **Regulatory Basis:** As per **GFR Rule 136**, the bill submitted by the supplier must be scrutinized for correctness, including tax details, before payment authorization.

*   **Why it's needed:** The existence of this security is a prerequisite. The final payment process involves ensuring this security is in place and will be released only after the contract is fully completed.
*   **Regulatory Basis:** **GFR Rule 176** deals with Security Deposits. It specifies that a Performance Security (usually a Bank Guarantee or Fixed Deposit Receipt) must be obtained from the supplier before making any advance payment or signing the contract.

*   **What it is:** A certificate from the concerned department stating whether any LD needs to be deducted from the supplier's bill due to delays in delivery or non-compliance with quality standards.
*   **Why it's needed:** If the supplier has defaulted on the contract terms, LD must be deducted as per the contract clause. If there are no defaults, a formal waiver is required.
*   **Regulatory Basis:** This is a mandatory contractual requirement. The deduction or waiver of LD must be clearly recorded and approved before the final bill is processed, ensuring adherence to the contract terms as per GFR principles.

### **5. Completion Certificate / Final Acceptance Letter**

*   **What it is:** A formal letter from the competent authority of the department certifying that the entire scope of work or supply has been completed as per the contract specifications.
*   **Why it's needed:** For the final, 100% payment, this certificate is often mandatory. It signifies that the supplier has fulfilled all their obligations.
*   **Regulatory Basis:** While the specific format is in the tender document, this aligns with the core principle of **GFR Rule 136**, which mandates acceptance of goods/services before payment.
