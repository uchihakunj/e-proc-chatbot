Q: What is the difference between Open Tender and Limited Tender?
Sources: FInal_GFR_upto_31_07_2024.pdf, Manual_for_Procurement_of_works_2019.pdf, CHiPS_Bid_Submission_Manual_English.pdf, Vigilance Manual (Updated 2021) English.pdf
Ans: ≡ƒÆí Answer
The primary difference between an Open Tender and a Limited Tender lies in their scope, publicity, and the list of eligible bidders. An Open Tender is advertised widely to invite competition from any interested party, while a Limited Tender is restricted to a pre-selected list of vendors.

| Feature | Open Tender | Limited Tender |
|---|---|---|
| **Bidding Process / Publicity** | Invitation to tenders by advertisement on GeM and CPPP. | Press publicity is not utilized. Bidders are directly intimated. |
| **Bidders** | Open to any interested bidder. | Restricted to a pre-selected list of vendors/contractors. |
| **Estimated Value** | For procurement of goods of estimated value of [Rs. 50 lakhs and above]. | For procurement of goods of estimated value up to [Rs. 5 lakhs] or for specific cases like limited sources, emergencies. |
| **Bidding Document Distribution** | Published on GeM and CPPP. | Sent directly by post/courier/e-mail to firms on the list of registered suppliers. |
| **Treatment of Unsolicited Bids** | Not specified, but generally considered. | Bids from uninvited bidders are treated as unsolicited and are normally not entertained. |

≡ƒôï Process
1.  **Determine the Estimated Value:** The first step is to assess the estimated value of the procurement.
2.  **Choose the Tender Method:**
    *   If the estimated value is [Rs. 50 lakhs and above], proceed with an Open Tender. The bidding documents must be advertised on GeM and the CPPP.
    *   If the estimated value is [up to Rs. 5 lakhs] or if the procurement is for limited sources, emergencies, or specific technical requirements, a Limited Tender is appropriate. The bidding documents are sent directly to the pre-selected list of vendors.

Rule/Provision
**Rule 161 Advertised Tender Enquiry (Open Tender):**
"invitation to tenders by advertisement should be used for procurement of goods of estimated value of [Rs. 50 lakhs (Rupees Fifty Lakh) and above. Advertisement in such cases should be given on GeM as well as on GeM- Central Public Procurement Portal (CPPP)."

**Rule 162 Limited Tender Enquiry:**
"This method may be adopted when estimated value of the goods to be procured is up to [Rupees Fifty Lakhs]. Copies of the bidding document should be sent directly by speed post/registered post/courier/ e-mail to firms which are borne on the list of registered suppliers for the goods in question as referred under Rule 150 above."

Explanation
An Open Tender is designed for higher-value procurements where the goal is to foster wide competition and ensure the best value for money. It relies on public advertisement to attract a large pool of bidders. In contrast, a Limited Tender is a more efficient and economical method for lower-value procurements or when the required skills, technology, or resources are specialized and limited to a known group of vendors. It is also used for emergency procurements or items with regular and repetitive use. The key distinction is the restriction of participation to a pre-qualified list in a Limited Tender, whereas an Open Tender is open to all.

Q: In which situations can Single Tender be used?
Sources: FInal_GFR_upto_31_07_2024.pdf, mannual procurement.pdf, Manual_for_Procurement_of_works_2019.pdf, publicProManual-1755343081262-715558279.pdf
Ans: ≡ƒÆí Answer
Single Tender can be used in the following situations:
1.  **Urgent Need:** There is an urgent need for the work, and engaging in a competitive tendering process would be impractical.
2.  **Natural Continuation:** The work represents a natural continuation of previous work, and the incremental work is not more than 25 (twenty-five) percent of the original contract value.
3.  **Emergency:** There is an emergency situation, such as after a natural disaster, where timely completion of the work is of utmost importance.
4.  **Proprietary Techniques/Expertise:** The work may involve the use of proprietary techniques or only one contractor has the requisite expertise.
5.  **National Security:** The procurement involves national defence or national security, and single source procurement is deemed most appropriate.
6.  **Single Manufacturer:** It is known that only a particular firm is the manufacturer of the required item, and equal opportunity is given to all bidders to participate in discussions.
7.  **Single Offer:** Only one responsive bid is received after a properly advertised tender, and the quoted price is reasonable.

≡ƒôï Process
1.  **Identify the Situation:** The procuring entity must first determine if the procurement meets one of the criteria for Single Tender.
2.  **Record and Approve Reasons:** The reasons for using Single Tender must be recorded and approved by the Competent Authority (CA) as per the delegation of powers.
3.  **Ensure Fairness and Equity:** The entity must ensure that prices are reasonable and consistent with market rates, and that the work is not split into smaller procurements.
4.  **Verify Single Offer:** If only one bid is received, the entity must verify that the procurement was satisfactorily advertised, sufficient time was given for bidding, and the price is reasonable.

Rule/Provision
i) There is an urgent need for the work and engaging in competitive tendering process would, therefore, be impractical, provided that the circumstances giving rise to the urgency were neither foreseeable by procuring entity nor the result of dilatory conduct on its part;
b) Works that represent a natural continuation of previous work carried out by the firm when considering the limited size of the additional work in relation to the original procurement and the reasonableness of the price it will be cost effective to resort to single source procurement. However, the incremental work should not be more than 25 (twenty-five) percent of the original contract value;

Explanation
Sometimes, against advertised/ limited tender cases, the procuring entity may not receive a sufficient number of bids and/ or after analysing the bids, ends up with only one responsive bid - a situation referred to as 'Single Offer'. As per Rule 21 of DFPR (explanation sub-para), such a situation of 'Single Offer' is to be treated as Single Tender. The contract may be placed on the 'Single Offer' bidder provided the quoted price is reasonable. However restricted powers of Single tender mode of procurement would apply. Even when only one bid is submitted, the process may be considered valid provided following conditions are satisfied: i) The procurement was satisfactorily advertised and sufficient time was given for submission of bids; ii) The qualification criteria were not unduly restrictive; and iii) Prices are reasonable in comparison to market values.

Q: Can Reverse Auction be used for every procurement?
Sources: Compilation of CVC Circulars and Guidelines.pdf, AuctionManual_FA.pdf, FInal_GFR_upto_31_07_2024.pdf, Vigilance Manual (Updated 2021) English.pdf, publicProManual-1755343081262-715558279.pdf
Ans: ≡ƒÆí Answer
No, Reverse Auction cannot be used for every procurement. It is not appropriate in certain situations, such as when the requirement is of low value, involves strategic or critical items, or when the quantity is to be split among multiple suppliers without sufficient suppliers available.

(Not applicable as the question is about appropriateness, not a procedure)

Rule/Provision
Reverse Auction would not be appropriate for:
i) The requirement is not of high enough value to generate competitive pressures on bidders.
ii) Items of strategic/ critical/ vital/ high technical complex nature, items that are in short supply in the market, or
iii) Where the QCBS s

Explanation
The documents state that caution is needed when using Reverse Auction. It is not suitable for repetitive/regularly procured items because future procurements may not see the same price reduction, making it difficult to justify higher rates later. It is also not appropriate for items of strategic, critical, vital, or high technical complexity, or items that are in short supply in the market. Furthermore, if a reverse auction is used to split an order quantity among more than one supplier, there must be an adequate number of suppliers available (at least N+3 if the quantity is split into N parts).

Ans: ≡ƒÆí Answer
Under the Chhattisgarh Store Purchase Rules, the following procurement methods are available for obtaining bids:

| Procurement Method |
|---|
| Advertised Tender Enquiry |
| Limited Tender Enquiry |
| Two-Stage Bidding |
| Single Tender Enquiry |
| Electronic Reverse Auctions |

≡ƒôï Process
The general process is to procure goods through a standard method of obtaining bids, as specified in the rules. This is the default approach for Ministries or Departments, except in cases covered under specific rules.

Rule/Provision:
Except in cases covered under Rule 154 and 155, Ministries or Departments shall procure goods under the powers referred to in Rule 140 above by following the standard method of obtaining bids in: 1. (i) Advertised Tender Enquiry 2. (ii) Limited Tender Enquiry 3. (iii) Two-Stage Bidding 4. (iv) Single Tender Enquiry 5. (v) Electronic Reverse Auctions

Explanation:
These methods are the standard procedures for obtaining bids, as mandated by the rules. The choice of method depends on the estimated value of the demand and other specific circumstances.

Q: When is the EMD refunded to the bidder?
Sources: Online_EMD_Refund_Notice.pdf, EMD_CHALLAN_PAYMENT_V1.0.pdf, Manual_for_Procurement_of_works_2019.pdf
Ans: ≡ƒÆí Answer
The EMD is refunded to the bidder based on their bid status and the subsequent approval process. For unsuccessful bidders, the EMD is returned through an e-payment system after the expiry of the final tender validity period, but not later than 30 days after the contract is concluded. For the successful bidder, the EMD is returned after the receipt of performance security. Once the department approves the refund process, the e-Procurement system sends an instruction to the bank, and the EMD amount is credited to the bidder's account within 1 to 2 days.

≡ƒôï Process
1.  The tender owner or department admin initiates the EMD refund process.
2.  The department approver verifies and approves the refund process.
3.  After approval, the e-Procurement system automatically sends an instruction to the bank to refund the EMD to the bidder's registered account.
4.  The EMD amount is credited to the respective bidder's account within 1 to 2 days after the approval and refund process.

Q: How do I register as a vendor on the Chhattisgarh e-Procurement portal?
Sources: FInal_GFR_upto_31_07_2024.pdf, mannual procurement.pdf, Manual_for_Procurement_of_works_2019.pdf, publicProManual-1755343081262-715558279.pdf
Ans: ≡ƒÆí Answer
Based on the provided documents, the registration process for a vendor involves registering on an e-procurement portal or the CPPP. The process requires capturing specific information and undergoing verification of credentials. Registration is typically for a fixed period and needs to be renewed.

≡ƒôï Process
1.  **Identify the Registration Portal:** All Ministries/Departments must register prospective contractors on their e-procurement portal or in the CPPP if they do not have their own.
2.  **Prepare Required Information:** The registration process requires capturing at least the following details:
    *   Name of contractor
    *   Address and Contact details
    *   Permanent Account Number (PAN)
    *   Details of digital signature certificate (DSC)
    *   GSTIN
3.  **Register and Verify:** The contractor's credentials, manufacturing capability, quality control systems, past performance, and financial background should be carefully verified before registration.
4.  **Understand Registration Period:** The supplier is registered for a fixed period, typically between 1 to 3 years, depending on the nature of the goods.
5.  **Renewal:** At the end of the registration period, the supplier must apply afresh for renewal.

Q: How do I submit my bid online after downloading the tender documents?
Sources: FInal_GFR_upto_31_07_2024.pdf, mannual procurement.pdf, Manual_for_Procurement_of_works_2019.pdf, publicProManual-1755343081262-715558279.pdf
Ans: ≡ƒÆí Answer
To submit your bid online, you must first register on the e-procurement portal, then download the tender documents, and finally upload the required bid documents in the specified formats. The tender fee and EMD are submitted as scanned copies, while the price bid (BOQ) must be uploaded in Excel format.

≡ƒôï Process
